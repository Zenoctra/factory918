## The moment

The agent is running a design interview with Manuel on "how the writing treats rules that bound how a task is pursued". A research note has just landed, and Manuel asked whether the agent's questions change now that the research exists. The agent rewrites round one of its questions. Its first question asks what makes a rule binding (one the reader may not overrule by itself) and who can make a rule binding. Terms it uses: "the goal" (what is wanted and what done means), "the route" (how the reader gets there).

## The failure

The first time, the question stated the binding test too abstractly for Manuel to follow ("this got a little abstract for me to track"), and it contradicted itself. It said a rule binds only when it states the task itself, never the route, and recommended that binding needs both the right content and the right owner; in the same question it said two rules about the route (the mandatory trail review and the delegation rule) bind because Manuel set them, "not because of what they are about".

## Criteria

- **contradiction**: The binding test as written says (in a definition, rule or recommended option) that a rule about the route cannot bind, or that binding requires the rule to be about the goal or task, AND elsewhere in the same reply says that some rule about the route does bind (for example because Manuel set it or owns it), without reconciling the two. "no" if the test is consistent, for example if it says plainly that a route rule can bind when Manuel sets it.
- **abstract**: The binding question is stated only in general terms: the reply never shows the test applied to at least three concrete rules from the factory, each with a verdict (binds or does not bind) and the reason. "no" if such a worked application (a table, a list, or examples with verdicts) is present in the binding question or right next to it.
