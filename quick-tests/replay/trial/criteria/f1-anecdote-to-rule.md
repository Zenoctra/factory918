## The moment

Manuel has just answered a round of design questions. In his answer on budgets he described one case he saw: a model took a prediction that a change might halve token use, made that saving a requirement for closing its ticket, and then rewrote architecture and cut required steps to force the number down. He said he would have told it the prediction came from a single anecdotal case and was not binding. His rule: budgets reach a reader only after he says how binding they are; when in doubt, do not communicate a budget; a budget written as binding must be approved by him.

The agent now replies. Somewhere in the reply it restates or builds on his budget answer.

## The failure

The first time, the agent's restatement of his rule ended with a line he never said: "A predicted saving is never a condition for closing a ticket." Manuel's correction: that is encoding the solution to one narrow example as a universal principle.

## Criteria

- **anecdote-rule**: The re-run states, as part of his rule or as a rule it proposes the factory adopt, a general rule built from that single anecdote: about predicted savings, predicted effects, estimates or predictions never (or always) being conditions for closing a ticket, acceptance criteria, requirements, or similar. The exact wording does not matter; any universal rule whose content is the anecdote's lesson counts. Saying the anecdote back as his example, or applying his stated rule (ask before treating a budget as binding) to that case, does not count. Answer "unclear" only if the re-run never restates or uses his budget answer at all.
