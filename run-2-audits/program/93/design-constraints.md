# Design constraints for #93 (owner lane, 2026-09-22, base d8e382c = PR #101's head)

Read beside `how/how.md` (the verified explanation of the round gate at this commit). This file lists what the ticket asks and what the code fixes. The design is the runner's.

## The ticket, verbatim where it decides

Title: "A happy-path fix gets another round; five rounds of them stop for a report".

Acceptance criteria:

1. A round whose Act on items include a Would-break fix is followed by another round, past three, up to five; a round whose fixes are all Fails-open items, prose, or standards breaches may be the last. `review-brief.sh` reads the kind of the fixed items from the previous round's comment and refuses a sixth round.
2. A round past three reviews the fix only: its fixed point is the commit the previous round reviewed, and the Ticket playbook and `spec-review` say so.
3. No rule compares hard-finding counts between rounds. The orchestrator's prose in `spec-review` step 5 says: at round three, when Would-break items are still being found, look for what is causing it; when the cause is not obvious, continue to five; at five with Would-break items still found, stop, write a report for the human on what is causing it, mark the PR blocked as unfinished, continue the work that does not depend on it, and wait for the human, since this is likely evidence the factory needs changing.
4. `babysit`'s merge-ready condition reads the same comment lines and is unchanged for a PR that ends at round three.
5. `tests/spec-review/` covers a round four with a fix-only fixed point and the round-five stop.
6. P20 amended; `docs/agents/review-ladder.md` says the same in one sentence.

Manuel's words (the ticket's Decision quotes): "never leave a hard bug change unreviewed"; a fail-open fix "can go unreviewed if its review 3 since that isnt all that dangerous"; "a hard bug in the happy path found implies maybe another should be run"; a rule comparing hard counts between rounds is "a hard pass" (rejected: reviews are not deterministic, one bug in round one and two in round two can be a perfect run); "If 5 reviews find hard bugs on the happy path, stop and investigate what is causing it and write a report for the user and continue the non blocked part of the job, but put a block here at this PR as unfinished"; "have it look for obvious issues at review 3 but if it doesnt immediately see what's causing it then go to 5 and check again. But wait until a human reviews your report since this is likely evidence the factory needs changed". The agent's addition, accepted on the ticket: "The extra round reviews only the fix, with the previously reviewed commit as the fixed point. The brief script already takes any commit as its fixed point, so this costs a fraction of a full round."

## Facts the design must fit (file:line in how/how.md)

- `review-comment.sh` ends the comment with: the summary line (`Standards: ...; Spec: ...; judged: act on A (X fixed, Y with a ticket), ...; fixed point <ref>.`), the bare line `restart` when any Act on item carries `hole:`, `round: N of 3` (N from `<dir>/round`, 1 when absent), `act-on items: K` last, K = |Act on| - |fixed:| - |ticket:| - |hole:| + |Ask|. Through `specs()` it can tell which report heading the report item behind an `[S<n>]`/`[P<n>]` reference sits under. It never reads earlier comments. It reads `<dir>/fixed-point`, `<dir>/round`, the two reports and the judgment, and nothing records the HEAD the round reviewed.
- `review-brief.sh` fetches the PR author's comments carrying `act-on items:`, cuts the history at the last `restart` comment, takes the round as 1 + the highest `round: N of 3` line (regex `^round: [0-9]+ of 3$`), refuses round 4 with one exact stderr message and exit 1 before any state, carries cited Noted/Dismissed items. `--round N` overrides the round; `--previous FILE` supplies comments from a file. It knows HEAD at brief time and writes `<dir>/fixed-point`, `<dir>/round`, `<dir>/log` (newest commit first), `<dir>/files`, `<dir>/diff`, `<dir>/stat`, then the two briefs.
- At rounds one and two the Act on items stay unfixed in the comment (K > 0) and are fixed after; the next round reviews the whole diff from the same fixed point. At round three a fix lane fixes them before the comment is posted, each is marked `fixed: <sha>`, K reads 0, and the PR is review-ready; babysit: "a comment reading `round: 3 of 3` and `act-on items: 0` makes the PR review-ready even when the fix commits it names come after the reviewed commit".
- A `fixed:` mark is allowed in any round (rounds one and two too); nothing today distinguishes what kind of finding was fixed.
- "Would-break" means the report item sits under `## Would break` in either report (Standards or Spec). A `## Fails open`, `## Standards breaches`, `## Fix alongside` or `## Not asked for` item is not Would-break, whatever the judgment says about it. A Would-break item the judgment Dismissed or Noted is not a fix.
- Babysit's condition lives in two vendored copies (`poteto-mode/playbooks/babysit.md` step 6 paragraph, `babysit/SKILL.md` step 4 bullets), changed through their patches, the same sentence in both; the ladder rung 1 and P20 say the cap in prose; `spec-review/SKILL.md` is vendored through `patches/mattpocock/spec-review.SKILL.md.patch`; `review-brief.sh`, `review-comment.sh` and `ticket.md` are the factory's own (`keep_files`).
- Tests: `tests/spec-review/review-brief.sh` builds comment histories with `pr me me:c1.md me:c2.md` through a fake `gh`, or `--previous FILE`, pins exact stdout with `printed`, substrings with `has`/`lacks`, and pins sentences of `SKILL.md` step 4 word for word. `tests/spec-review/review-comment.sh` pins exact comment tails with `accept`, exact refusals with `refuse`; `rearm()` writes the state files a fixture needs. `tests/spec-review/no-stale-wording.sh` greps `template/` and `docs/knowledge/core/` for retired phrases. Every existing assertion keeps passing unless a cell says otherwise, and then the cell says which.
- The sweep form (`--paths ... --commits ...`) has no PR rounds beyond what the comments give; unchanged unless a cell needs it.
- Ticket #90's table A (comment history down the side; `gh` default, `--round N`, `--previous FILE` across) is the precedent for the brief's table; its table B (report item down the side, judgment mark across) for the comment script's table. A cell reads "prints / exit / what the caller does". A cell for an input outside the intended path reads "refused with the tool's own message" and costs no code; it is cut only after the refusal was run and seen.

## Questions the design must answer (each becomes a cell or a contract line)

1. How does round N's comment carry, for round N+1's brief and for babysit, (a) whether a Would-break item was fixed in round N and (b) the commit round N reviewed (the fix-only fixed point)? A new line, a changed line, or parsed from the judgment and reports already in the comment? A PR that ends at round three must read exactly as today (`round: 3 of 3`, `act-on items: 0`). Where does the reviewed commit come from (the brief knows HEAD; the comment script runs after the fixes were committed)?
2. What does the `round:` line say in rounds four and five? What does the brief print, and what does it refuse (exact messages), for: round 4 asked after a round-3 comment without a Would-break fix; round 4 after one with; round 5 after a round-4 with and without; round 6; `--round 4` or `--round 5` with `--previous`; `--round N` with no comments; a fixed point at round 4 that is not the commit round 3 reviewed (enforced by the script with a refusal, or by the playbook only, and why)?
3. At round five with Would-break items still found: what does the comment carry so babysit reads the PR as unfinished (not ready, and not a blocker to fix here), and what does the orchestrator do (the report for the human, the block on the PR)? Where does the "look for what is causing it at three, continue to five when not obvious" prose live (step 5) and how does it avoid becoming a count-comparison rule (criterion 3)?
4. A Would-break item marked `fixed:` in round one or two: does the next round differ from today? (Criterion 1 says any such round is followed by another round; rounds two and three review from the original fixed point as today.) A Would-break item fixed in round one with `act-on items: 0`: is the PR ready? What does babysit read?
5. A rebuilt comment in the same round (an Ask answered, `review-comment.sh <dir>` rerun): which comment of round N decides for round N+1? A restart comment that also names a Would-break fix: which wins?
6. Which prose changes, exactly: `spec-review/SKILL.md` steps 1, 5 and 6; `ticket.md` (which step or section says the fix-only fixed point and the round-five stop); both babysit copies (one sentence, byte-identical in both); the ladder rung 1 (one sentence); P20 (amended in place with a dated clause; the precedent is "Amended 2026-09-18 after #77"); `MANUAL.md`'s merge checklist line; the scripts' header comments; `SOURCES.md` items 4, 6, 12; `no-stale-wording.sh` (which retired phrase: for instance "at most three rounds").
7. `<dir>/head` or whatever file carries the reviewed commit: what does `review-comment.sh` do when it is absent (older `review-brief.sh`, a fixture)? Refuse, or print without it? Direction of failure decides.

## Shape of the hand-back

A design package per `.claude/skills/architect/references/rationale-template.md` (usage first, then the shape), and inside it the scenario table(s) in ticket #90's shape (a `Terms` paragraph, a legend, table A for `review-brief.sh` over the comment history, table B for `review-comment.sh` over one round's judgment if the comment changes) with a `Contract` section under them (every term the cells use, the exact new or changed lines, the exact refusal messages, the prose to add per file as the text to add), a "Tests, one assertion per cell" list naming the fixture state each needs, and a "Criteria map" from the six criteria to cells and contract lines. Prefer the smallest diff that satisfies the six criteria; a new machine line in the comment is fine if it is the smallest honest encoding; changing the meaning of an existing line is fine if every reader is updated and criterion 4 still holds.
