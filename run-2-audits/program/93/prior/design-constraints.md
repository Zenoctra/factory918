# Design constraints for #93 (written by the owner lane, 2026-09-22)

Read with `how.md` beside this file. This lists what the ticket asks and what the code fixes; the design is yours.

## What the ticket asks (the six criteria, verbatim)

1. A round whose Act on items include a Would-break fix is followed by another round, past three, up to five; a round whose fixes are all Fails-open items, prose, or standards breaches may be the last. `review-brief.sh` reads the kind of the fixed items from the previous round's comment and refuses a sixth round.
2. A round past three reviews the fix only: its fixed point is the commit the previous round reviewed, and the Ticket playbook and `spec-review` say so.
3. No rule compares hard-finding counts between rounds. The orchestrator's prose in `spec-review` step 5 says: at round three, when Would-break items are still being found, look for what is causing it; when the cause is not obvious, continue to five; at five with Would-break items still found, stop, write a report for the human on what is causing it, mark the PR blocked as unfinished, continue the work that does not depend on it, and wait for the human, since this is likely evidence the factory needs changing.
4. `babysit`'s merge-ready condition reads the same comment lines and is unchanged for a PR that ends at round three.
5. `tests/spec-review/` covers a round four with a fix-only fixed point and the round-five stop.
6. P20 amended; `docs/agents/review-ladder.md` says the same in one sentence.

Manuel's words that decide it (from the ticket): "never leave a hard bug change unreviewed"; a fail-open fix "can go unreviewed if its review 3"; "a hard bug in the happy path found implies maybe another should be run"; a rule comparing hard counts between rounds "is a hard pass" (rejected); "If 5 reviews find hard bugs on the happy path, stop and investigate what is causing it and write a report for the user and continue the non blocked part of the job, but put a block here at this PR as unfinished"; "have it look for obvious issues at review 3 but if it doesnt immediately see what's causing it then go to 5 and check again. But wait until a human reviews your report". The agent's addition: "The extra round reviews only the fix, with the previously reviewed commit as the fixed point."

## Facts the design must fit (see how.md for file:line)

- Today `review-comment.sh` ends the comment with: the summary line (`...; fixed point <ref>.`), the bare line `restart` when any Act on item carries `hole:`, `round: N of 3` (N from `<dir>/round`), `act-on items: K` last, where K = |Act on| - |fixed:| - |ticket:| - |hole:| + |Ask|. It can tell which report heading an Act on item's `[S<n>]`/`[P<n>]` rests under (the `specs()` walk yields heading per item in document order). It never reads earlier comments.
- Today `review-brief.sh` fetches the PR author's comments carrying `act-on items:`, cuts at the last `restart` line, takes the round as 1 + the highest `round: N of 3`, refuses round 4 (`review-brief: three rounds were run on this PR; the remaining Act on items are fixed here and marked \`fixed: <sha>\`, not reviewed in a fourth round`, stderr, exit 1, no state), carries cited Noted/Dismissed items as settled. `--round N` overrides the round; `--previous FILE` supplies comments from a file. It records nothing about which commit (HEAD) a round reviewed; the fixed point is the caller's argument (`git diff <fixed>...HEAD`). On PR #92 every round used `fixed point origin/main`.
- In rounds one and two the Act on items are left unfixed in the comment (K > 0) and fixed after, and the next round reviews the whole diff from the same fixed point. At round three the fix lane fixes them before the comment is posted, they are marked `fixed: <sha>`, K reads 0, and the PR is review-ready ("`round: 3 of 3` and `act-on items: 0` makes the PR review-ready even when the fix commits it names come after the reviewed commit", babysit).
- "Would-break" means an item under `## Would break` in either report (Standards or Spec). Fails-open items, Standards breaches, Fix alongside, Not asked for are not Would-break. A Would-break item the judgment Dismissed or put under Noted is not a fix.
- Babysit's condition lives in two vendored copies (`poteto-mode/playbooks/babysit.md` step 6 paragraph, `babysit/SKILL.md` step 4 bullet), changed through their patches, with the same sentence in both; the ladder rung 1 and P20 say the cap in prose. `spec-review/SKILL.md` is vendored through `patches/mattpocock/spec-review.SKILL.md.patch`; `review-brief.sh`, `review-comment.sh` and `ticket.md` are the factory's own (`keep_files`).
- The tests: `tests/spec-review/review-brief.sh` builds comment histories with `pr me me:c1.md me:c2.md` through a fake `gh`, or `--previous FILE`, and pins exact stdout with `printed`, substrings with `has`/`lacks`; it also pins that `SKILL.md` step 4 carries certain sentences word for word. `tests/spec-review/review-comment.sh` pins exact comment tails. Every existing assertion should keep passing unless a cell says otherwise; `tests/spec-review/no-stale-wording.sh` greps `template/` and `docs/knowledge/core/` for retired phrases.
- The sweep form (`--paths ... --commits ...`) has no PR and no rounds beyond what the comments give; keep it unchanged unless a cell needs it.
- Ticket #90's table A (rows on the comment history, columns on the input shape: default `gh`, `--round N`, `--previous FILE`) is the precedent for the brief's table; its table B (report item down the side, judgment mark across) is the precedent for the comment script's table. A cell reads "prints / exit / what the caller does". A cell for an input outside the intended path reads "refused with the tool's own message" and costs no code.

## Questions the design must answer (each becomes cells or contract)

1. How does round N's comment carry, for round N+1's brief, (a) whether a Would-break item was fixed in round N and (b) the commit round N reviewed (the fix-only fixed point)? A new line, a changed line, or parsed from the judgment and reports already in the comment? Babysit reads the same lines: a PR that ends at round three must read exactly as today (`round: 3 of 3`, `act-on items: 0`).
2. What does the `round:` line say in rounds four and five? What does the brief print, and what does it refuse (exact messages), for: round 4 asked after a round-3 comment without a Would-break fix; round 4 after one with; round 5 after a round-4 with/without; round 6; `--round 4` or `--round 5` with `--previous`; `--round N` with no comments; a fixed point at round 4 that is not the commit round 3 reviewed (enforced by the script, or by the playbook only?).
3. At round five with a Would-break fix: what does the comment carry so babysit reads the PR as unfinished (not ready, not a blocker to fix here), and what does the orchestrator do (the report for the human, the block on the PR)? Where does the "look for what is causing it at three, continue to five when not obvious" prose live (step 5) and how does it avoid becoming a count-comparison rule (criterion 3)?
4. A Would-break item marked `fixed:` in round one or two (allowed today, "the round-three path" is a comment, not a check): does it change anything? Rounds one to three still review from the original fixed point.
5. A rebuilt comment in the same round (an Ask answered, `review-comment.sh <dir>` rerun): which comment of round N decides for round N+1?
6. Which prose changes, exactly: `spec-review/SKILL.md` steps 1, 5 and 6; `ticket.md` (which step says the fix-only fixed point); both babysit copies (one sentence, byte-identical in both); the ladder rung 1 (one sentence); P20 (amended in place with a dated clause, the precedent is "Amended 2026-09-18 after #77"); the scripts' header comments; `SOURCES.md` items 4, 6, 12; `no-stale-wording.sh` (which retired phrase, e.g. "at most three rounds").

## Shape of the hand-back

A design package per `architect/references/rationale-template.md` (usage first, then the shape), and inside it the scenario table(s) in ticket #90's shape with a `Contract` section under them, plus a "Tests, one assertion per cell" list and a "Criteria map". Prefer the smallest diff that satisfies the six criteria; a new machine line in the comment is fine if it is the smallest honest encoding; changing the meaning of an existing line is fine if every reader is updated and the criterion-4 sentence still holds.
