### Terms

A "comment" is a comment on the PR by its author whose body has a line starting `act-on items:` (the jq filter at `review-brief.sh:100`); anything else is never fetched and appears in no cell. A "restart comment" is a comment with a line that is exactly `restart` outside fenced text (#90). A "history" is the comments after the last restart comment, oldest first. The "deciding comment" is the last comment of the history: the one posted last, so a comment rebuilt in the same round (an Ask answered) decides in place of the one it rebuilt. The "last-round path" is what round three does today and rounds four and five do too: the fix lane fixes the Act on items on this PR before the comment is posted, and the judgment marks each `fixed: <sha>`. A "Would-break fix" is an Act on judgment item whose line ends in a `fixed: <sha>` field (the ending rule of #90: a `hole:` before a trailing `fixed:` is text) and whose report item, found through its `[S<n>]`/`[P<n>]` reference the way `specs()` finds it for `hole:`, sits under `## Would break` in the Standards or the Spec report; a fixed item under `## Fails open`, `## Standards breaches`, `## Fix alongside` or `## Not asked for` is not one, and a Would-break item that is Noted, Dismissed, under Ask or Consider, ticketed, or marked `hole:` with no trailing `fixed:` is not one. The "reviewed commit" of a round is `HEAD` when that round's `review-brief.sh` ran, the tip of the diff it briefed; the script records it once, as the full 40-hex id, in `<dir>/reviewed`, and it reaches the next round only through the comment. The "WB line" is the line `would-break fixed after <sha>` (`<sha>` 40 lowercase hex) outside fenced text; a comment carries at most one, and when a quoted hunk holds one too, the last such line in the comment is its own, as for `round:`. A "fix-only round" is a round past three: its fixed point is the sha of the deciding comment's WB line, so `git diff <sha>...HEAD` and `git log <sha>..HEAD` are the fix commits and nothing else. The "cap" printed in `round: N of <cap>` is 3, or 5 for rounds four and five and for a round-three comment carrying the WB line, so `of 5` reads: a Would-break fix extended the review. A history is written oldest first; `(3W s)` is a round-three comment carrying `would-break fixed after s`, `(3)` one without, `(4W t)` likewise, `(1W s)` a round-one comment with a Would-break fix; `o` is the original fixed point (`origin/main`), `s`, `t` the shas of the WB lines, `x` any other ref.

### Contract

**The three calls.** `scripts/review-brief.sh <fixed-point> [--ticket N] ...` (round one to three: the fixed point the user pinned, as today); `scripts/review-brief.sh <sha> --ticket N` (round four or five: `<sha>` the deciding comment's WB line, `--ticket N` because the fix commits may not name the ticket); `scripts/review-comment.sh [<dir>]` unchanged in its arguments. Exit codes as today: 0 the briefs written, 1 refused before any state with the message on stderr.

**The reviewed commit, recorded once.** `review-brief.sh` writes `git rev-parse HEAD` to `<dir>/reviewed` on the line after `echo "$round" > "$dir/round"` (`:266`), in both forms (the sweep form writes it too, one code path; it is never read there, table A row 16). Nothing else writes it. `review-comment.sh` reads it only when a Would-break fix exists (table B rows 3 to 5, 7), validates `^[0-9a-f]{40}$`, and prints it in the WB line; `review-brief.sh` reads it back from the deciding comment. The brief knows `HEAD` at brief time, which is the tip it diffs; the comment script runs after the fix lane committed, when `HEAD` is past the reviewed tip, so the value crosses from the brief to the comment through the dir and from the comment to the next brief through the PR. Derived from one record, never synced.

**The WB line.** `review-comment.sh`, after `fixed_here` (`:214`): a loop over `items "$dir/judgment.md" "Act on" | grep -E 'fixed: [0-9a-f]{7,40}$'`, each line's `[S<n>]`/`[P<n>]` resolved to its report and heading with the two lines `holed`'s loop already uses (`specs "$f" | sed -n "${ref_id#[SP]}p"`, `${rests%%$'\t'*}`); `wb_fixed` = the number whose heading is `Would break`. When `wb_fixed` is nonzero: `RM` when `<dir>/reviewed` is missing, `RB` when its first line is not 40 lowercase hex, else the line `would-break fixed after <sha>` printed after the `restart` line and before `round:`. The cap: `cap=3`, and `cap=5` when `round` is 4 or more, or `round` is 3 and `wb_fixed` is nonzero; `echo "round: $round of $cap"`. `act-on items:` stays last, its count unchanged.

## Problem

The review runs three rounds and round three's fixes go out unreviewed whatever they are; PR #87 shipped a behavior-changing fix for a happy-path bug that way. Manuel's rule is that a fix for a hard bug on the documented path is never left unreviewed, a fail-open fix may be, and no rule may compare hard counts between rounds. The shape is non-obvious because the comment is the record: `review-brief.sh` rebuilds the round from the PR's comments and `review-comment.sh` deletes the working state, so any fact round N+1 needs, the kind of the fixed items and the commit round N reviewed, has to be written into round N's comment, and nothing today records the reviewed commit (the comment prints the user's fixed-point ref, `<dir>/log` goes only into the briefs). Constraints from grounding: a PR that ends at round three must print today's lines, so the twenty-two `accept` and fourteen `printed` pins stay; the two babysit copies carry one sentence byte for byte and change through patches; `specs()` already maps a judgment reference to its report heading but is only called on the `hole:` path; `F4`'s text is pinned three times.

## Shape

Data first. One new record, `<dir>/reviewed`, the reviewed commit's full id, written once by the brief beside `<dir>/round` and read by the comment script; one new comment line, `would-break fixed after <sha>`, that carries two facts the next round needs (a Would-break item was fixed; the commit it was fixed after) and one that babysit needs (a round is owed, or at five the human's line); one derived digit, the cap in `round: N of <cap>`, so the round line stays true (`3 of 5` reads: a hard-bug fix extended the review). The flow: brief writes `reviewed` → judgment marks `fixed:` → comment resolves each fixed item's heading through `specs()` (the same two lines the `hole:` check uses, now on the `fixed:` path) → prints the line → next brief cuts the deciding comment, reads the line, gates the round, requires the fixed point, pastes the fixed items → the diff is the fix.

Load-bearing decisions. (1) The kind of the fixed item is read from the report heading, not from a new judgment field: the judgment already names the report item, `specs()` already knows its heading, so "Would-break" is derived, never written twice (single source of truth). (2) The reviewed commit is recorded at the only moment it is known (brief time) and travels through the comment, because the comment is the record and the next round may run in a fresh context (P27 rejected a state file for the same reason). (3) The line is conditional, so a PR that ends at round three is byte-identical and the pinned tails hold (criterion 4); the cap digit is derived from the same facts, not a second record. (4) The gate is in the script: the fixed point past three must be the reviewed commit, because a wrong one fails toward the wrong review (a later commit skips part of the fix, which is the bug this ticket exists to close; the original fixed point re-reviews the whole diff at full cost); the playbook says the rule in words, the script holds it (boundary discipline: validate at the call, trust the dir inside). (5) `F4` keeps its text so no existing assertion moves for it; `F5` and `F6` are its siblings. (6) `FT` closes the one silent path the fix-only round opens: fix commits that name no ticket would drop the Spec axis without a word in the round that reviews a hard-bug fix. (7) Rounds one to three are untouched by the gate (row 12): the line there is a sign to babysit and nothing else, so the whole-diff rounds keep their fixed point. (8) Restart wins over the line (row 13): the slice removes it, and the redesign's round one reviews the fix inside the whole diff.

Interface depth. The public surface grows by one optional line in the comment and one fixed-point rule for the caller; behind it the scripts hide the heading lookup, the deciding-comment cut, the sha check, the cap, the fix section and six refusals. The orchestrator learns one thing: pass the sha the comment names. What stays exposed on purpose: the two stops in step 5 are prose, because Manuel asked for judgment, not a rule, and a count rule was rejected.

What the design deliberately does not do: no count of Would-break items in the comment (a count invites the comparison criterion 3 forbids); no `reviewed:` line on every comment (criterion 4); no state outside the review dir; no change to `act-on items:`, to the summary line, to `settled:` or to the restart slice; no fix-only round in the sweep form.

## Tradeoffs accepted

- We accept one conditional machine line in the comment in exchange for byte-identical tails on every PR that ends at round three; the alternative, a `reviewed:` line always, would move every pinned tail.
- We accept `round: 3 of 5` on a round-three comment with a Would-break fix, a digit that depends on the judgment, in exchange for a round line that never reads "the last of three" when a fourth is owed and a babysit sentence that stays true without an exception clause.
- We accept a refusal (`RM`/`RB`) in the comment script for a missing or malformed `<dir>/reviewed` only when a Would-break fix exists, in exchange for every existing fixture printing as today; the refusal tells the caller to write the commit by hand rather than rerun the brief, because the brief rerun after the fixes would record the wrong commit.
- We accept `FT` (a fix-only round with no ticket resolvable is refused when the previous round had a spec) and the `--ticket N` in the usage, in exchange for never dropping the Spec axis silently in the round that reviews a hard-bug fix; reading the ticket from the PR's closing references was left out as a wider change.
- We accept that round five's Would-break fixes are made and go unreviewed (the same last-round path as three and four) in exchange for a PR that never carries a known hard bug unfixed; the line at `round: 5 of 5` and the draft mark are the block, the report is what the human reads.
- We accept a new brief section (`## The fix under review`) in exchange for a fix-only reviewer who knows what the diff claims to fix; the commit messages alone were judged too thin a carrier.
- We accept three moved assertions, all in fixtures that already marked a Would-break item `fixed:`, in exchange for no special case in the script for "an old fixture".

## Alternatives considered

- **A `reviewed: <sha>` line on every comment, and a Would-break count in the summary line.** Exposes the reviewed commit everywhere (babysit's "on the latest commit" could become mechanical) but changes every comment, moves every `accept` and `printed` pin, and puts a count in the record that criterion 3 says nothing may compare. Lost on criterion 4 and on the count.
- **Keep `round: N of 3` for rounds one to three always and let the WB line alone say a round is owed.** Smaller by one derived digit, but a round-three comment would read `round: 3 of 3` and `act-on items: 0` with a fourth round owed, so babysit's existing sentence would need an exception clause and the ladder's "3 of 3 makes the PR review-ready" would be false as written. Lost on legibility of the record.
- **Rename the cap to `of 5` everywhere.** One regex, one string, but every pinned `round: N of 3` moves and a PR that ends at round three no longer reads as today. Lost on criterion 4.
- **Derive the reviewed commit at comment time from the earliest `fixed:` commit's parent, no `<dir>/reviewed`.** No new file, but the fix lane may commit other things first, a `fixed:` sha may be a short id, and the round-three brief already knows `HEAD` exactly; deriving from the marks is a guess where a record is free. Lost on single source of truth.
- **Enforce the fix-only fixed point in the playbook only.** No `FP`/`FR`, but a wrong fixed point at four silently reviews too much or, worse, too little. Lost on direction of failure.
- **A judgment field (`kind: would-break`) instead of reading the report heading.** A second statement of a fact the report already states, checked or not; the `hole:` check already reads the heading through `specs()`. Lost on derive-not-sync.
- **Leave round five's Would-break items unfixed, the nonzero count as the block.** No draft mark needed, but babysit reads a nonzero count as a blocker to fix on this PR, the opposite of criterion 3's wait, and the PR would carry a known hard bug. Lost on criterion 3.

## Open questions and risks

- Is `gh pr ready --undo` (draft) the right mark for "blocked as unfinished", or would Manuel rather see a label or a title prefix? Does the git guard hook let `gh pr ready` through, and does the watcher treat a draft frontier as a wait rather than a red check? If draft is wrong, the block is still the WB line at `round: 5 of 5` in the last review comment, which babysit reads as a wait; only the visible mark changes.
- Should round five's fix lane fix at all, or should the findings stand for the human? The design fixes and marks (no known hard bug left on the PR) and the report names which fixes went unreviewed; the other reading is defensible.
- `--ticket N` at rounds four and five: keep `FT` and the explicit flag, or have `review-brief.sh` read the PR's `closingIssuesReferences` when the commits name no ticket? The latter is a general improvement and a wider change to the fake `gh`.
- Is `## The fix under review` worth its paragraph, or is the commit list enough for a fix-only reviewer? It is the one part of this design that is a reviewer aid rather than a gate; cutting it removes one `has` pin and one step 4 bullet and changes no cell.
- Risk: a hand-edited history through `--previous` can carry a WB line whose sha resolves but is not the commit the previous round reviewed; the script cannot know, and the test-only path is trusted as today.
- Risk: the three moved assertions are exactly the fixtures where a Would-break item was marked `fixed:`; a writer who "fixes" them by dropping the mark instead of adding the line and the file would pass the old expectation and fail the cell. The test list says which way they move.
