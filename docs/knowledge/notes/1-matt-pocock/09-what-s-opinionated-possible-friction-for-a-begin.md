## What's opinionated / possible friction for a beginner (honest, sourced)

1. **It assumes you can make engineering decisions.** Grilling is "a conversation, not an interview"; "Passivity allows agents to overwhelm you with excessive questions and scope explosion" ([9 things](https://www.aihero.dev/things-people-get-wrong-with-grill-me-and-grill-with-docs)); "what comes out tracks the quality of your answers, not the number of questions asked" ([docs/productivity/grill-me.md](research/1-matt-pocock/skills-repo/docs/productivity/grill-me.md)). The seam-choice prompt in `/tdd` "lists candidate seams by name only" (#607) — a beginner will not know which to pick; the documented workaround is to ask the agent for the trade-offs first. Matt's own composition for this case: `/handoff` to a `/teach` workspace, learn, come back.
2. **Question volume and token cost.** "Forty-six questions across four rounds is an ordinary session"; "It asked me two hundred questions... Usually the scope was too large." Caps are refused by design. A single `/implement` ticket over 100k tokens "is normal rather than a sign something broke". Reviews "find new stuff every time... There is no convergence guarantee."
3. **Model dependency.** "Grilling requires models with substantial parametric knowledge. 'A dumb model won't give you good ideas.'" Weaker models skip the confirmation gate and start building; `/diagnosing-bugs` over-fires on some non-Claude models.
4. **Many things the flow does not do:** `/implement` won't open a PR, close tickets, or act on review findings; `/to-tickets` won't dispatch; setup won't create labels; no global config; no `~/.claude` mode; no evals of the skills; the router "lags the repo" and can misdescribe skills.
5. **Documented bugs still open (as of the snapshot):** `grill-with-docs` sometimes fails to load its two dependencies; `code-review` name collision and recursive sub-agents; `research` self-nesting (~450k tokens once); `teach` writing into the skill folder and quiz answers always "A"; `improve-codebase-architecture` grilling instead of reporting; wayfinder agents writing production code mid-map; sub-issues/blocking links not created on GitHub.
6. **TypeScript/Node lean.** Misc skills (`shoehorn`, Husky, exercises) are TS-only; the deep-module enforcement answer is dependency-cruiser/Effect. The core spine is language-agnostic. Secondhand: "These encode 'how Matt Pocock engineers — a TypeScript-heavy, library-and-teaching-oriented practice.'" ([alexrusin](https://blog.alexrusin.com/agentic-coding-pipeline-matt-pocock-skills/))
7. **Ceremony tax on small changes.** "For a solo dev changing a form validation rule, running the full spine costs more than the change." ([alexrusin](https://blog.alexrusin.com/agentic-coding-pipeline-matt-pocock-skills/), opinion) Matt's own docs agree: "the spec earns its step only on multi-session work... Go grilling → `/implement`."
8. **Unverified effectiveness claims.** A critical guide notes "'sounds more sophisticated' and 'is more effective' are different claims" and recommends cherry-picking rather than installing everything ([kaizencode.art, 2026-07-16](https://kaizencode.art/notepad/matt-pocock-skills-guide/), opinion). Governance critique: "Good skills encode judgment. Bad skills encode superstition." ([developersdigest.tech, 2026-05-14](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/skills-for-real-engineers-governance), opinion)
9. **Plugin read-only-ness cuts both ways.** You cannot edit a plugin skill; "Put standing behaviour in your own `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md`, or say it in the invocation." Editing skills.sh copies gets overwritten by `npx skills update`.
10. **Stale community guides abound.** Anything mentioning `/caveman`, `/diagnose`, `write-a-prd`, `to-prd`, `to-issues`, or "28 skills" (e.g. the [devtalk review](https://devtalk.com/t/anyone-tried-mattpocock-skills-for-claude-code-here-is-what-i-found-after-a-week/244771), the [tosea.ai guide](https://tosea.ai/blog/matt-pocock-skills-claude-code-guide), the DeepLearningAI fork) describes the pre-1.0 repo. Use the CHANGELOG to translate.

**How people experience it (secondhand, labelled):** the devtalk reviewer (pre-1.0) found `/grill-with-docs` made "Every session after that... noticeably better" and advised "Start with 4... Add more later if needed"; andrew.ooo (May 2026) reported "time-to-correct-PR was consistently 20-40% lower with the skills installed" and "Skip a week of edits and the agent's accuracy regresses fast" about `CONTEXT.md`; alexrusin (Aug 2026): "The grilling step and the written-artifact discipline earn their keep immediately, the spec-to-tickets split earns it only on work large enough to span sessions, and the rest is situational."
